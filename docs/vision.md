# Vision and principles

Updated: 2026-10-05. Status: design documentation; no game implementation yet.

## Confirmed

Original persistent browser strategy inspired by Travian and BFME. Kingdom defence and village-territory conquest replace independent alliances. Player identity is master blacksmith, with full control of their own settlement resources, recruitment and military actions. Named kingdom rulers are separate characters, likely unique heroes.

The central experience is warfare, conquest and building cool armies equipped with powerful weapons. The master-blacksmith identity is the theme and framing; smithing is a tool for equipping and strengthening armies. The game does not need to make every action feel like a blacksmith activity or organise all play around a crafting simulation.

Kingdoms need distinct, viable rosters with strengths and weaknesses. Ordinary forging and later runeforging support army progression and military choices. Start medieval and evolve gradually.

## Core loop direction

Develop villages and resources -> recruit and equip armies -> fight, defend and conquer territory -> use growth and opportunities to strengthen armies. This expresses the confirmed feel, not a final rule for conquest rewards, village limits or victory.

## Accepted direction

Village-centred districts, useful kingdom duties, differentiated camps, regiment identity, pre-battle stances, fortress modules and capture objectives. These require concrete rules and first-version scoping.

## Proposed experience goals

Useful support/defensive participation, recovery after defeat, understandable combat, manageable attendance, mobile usability and no purchased combat power. A 20–40 minute daily target was suggested but not confirmed.

## Design risks

Unrewarding battles or conquest; army compositions and equipment upgrades that do not feel useful; dominant faction choices; empty fronts in a small alpha; endless elimination; fantasy or naval scope entering the first version prematurely. Validate these with playable tests. Evaluate forging by how it supports building and strengthening armies, not by whether it dominates play.
